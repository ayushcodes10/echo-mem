## Why this exists

Echo Memory is Apache-2.0 and intended to stay that way. Contributors keep the
copyright in everything they write; this agreement grants a licence, it does not
transfer ownership.

What it adds beyond the Apache-2.0 licence on the repository is the right to
distribute your contribution under a **different** licence later. That matters
for one specific and honest reason: the project may eventually offer a hosted
commercial edition, and some parts of it — most likely the team and organisation
features — may ship under a source-available licence rather than Apache-2.0.

Without this agreement, changing the licence on any file would require tracking
down and getting written permission from every person who ever touched it.
Projects that skipped this step and later needed to move have had to rewrite
contributions from scratch or abandon the change. Asking up front is the version
of that conversation where nobody is surprised.

**What this does not do.** It does not let the maintainer take your contribution
proprietary and leave you without it: everything you contribute remains
available to you under the Apache-2.0 licence it was accepted under, and that
grant is irrevocable. It does not stop you from using your own work however you
like. It does not give up your copyright.

## How to sign

You do not need to send anything by email or print anything. Open a pull
request; a bot will comment asking you to reply with the sentence it gives you.
That reply is your signature, and it is recorded once — subsequent pull requests
do not ask again.
