## Problem Statement

Echo Memory is being submitted to Y Combinator at pre-product stage: 154 facts,
43 `write_episode` calls, 9 days of history, 0 external users, 0 stars, 0 forks.
The application has to carry weight on founder, insight and velocity, because it
cannot carry weight on traction. This session decided what the application leads
with and what the founder does before submitting.

## Demand Evidence

Weak, and named honestly rather than dressed up.

- **Founder's own usage is the strongest signal.** 154 facts across 43 writes in
  9 days is behaviour, not opinion. Founder-as-user is a legitimate pattern.
- **2-3 friends "showed interest".** Zero installed. Interest is not demand:
  selection bias, social pressure, zero cost to say yes.
- **"A lot of people demand this online."** This argues against, not for. That
  demand is already served - Mem0 has 51k stars, $24M raised, 100k+ developers.
  Category demand is proven; demand for *this* is not.
- **0 stars after 9 days public.** The founder diagnosed this as distribution
  (nobody knows it exists) rather than positioning or product. That is now a
  falsifiable claim: post it in three places and see.

By the project's own instrumentation, criterion 6 has never returned a reading,
so there is no measured evidence the recall loop saves anyone anything yet.

## Status Quo

The real competitor is **a hand-written `CLAUDE.md` or `.cursorrules` file**, not
Mem0. It is free, zero-setup, and works. Its costs: it goes stale silently, it
does not cross tools, and it does not scale past a few dozen facts.

Note the trap in the founder's framing: "never forgets" is not the differentiator
against a file, because a file never forgets either. The stronger and true claim
is **"knows what is still true"** - Echo Memory timestamps facts and invalidates
the ones that changed, which a markdown file cannot do.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Unresolved, and this is the session's central finding.**

The founder could not name a specific human (answered "someone making chatbot", a
category). More importantly, that single external interest signal points at a
*different product* than the one being built:

| | Coding-agent memory (built) | Chatbot memory (the interest) |
|---|---|---|
| Who remembers | the developer's tools | the app's end users |
| Cross-tool | the entire pitch | irrelevant, there is one agent |
| Write volume | 43 facts in 9 days | thousands per day |
| Competitor | Cognee, claude-mem | Mem0's core market |

**The zero-inference write path is worth roughly 100x more in the chatbot market.**
At 43 facts in 9 days, Zep's 3-calls-per-episode costs ~129 calls, which is
nothing. At chatbot volume it is the line item someone gets fired over. The
differentiator is marginal in the targeted market and decisive in the untargeted
one.

This did NOT trigger a pivot, deliberately. See Premises and Recommended Approach.

## Premises

All five confirmed by the founder.

1. The differentiator is **write cost**, not the graph. Zep has the same
   bi-temporal shape and a published paper.
2. There is **no demand evidence beyond the founder's own usage**.
3. **The one-line pitch cannot be written yet**, because it comes from users'
   words and there are no users.
4. The only external interest **points at chatbot memory, not coding agents**.
5. At pre-product YC judges founders and insight over traction, but **zero users
   plus an undemonstrated core claim is a weak pairing**.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Hold the coding-agent wedge, fix distribution — CHOSEN
Ship where the build is aimed. Post in three places, convert one friend to an
install, let criterion 6 return its first real reading, apply on founder plus
insight plus velocity. Effort S (human ~1 wk / CC ~2 hrs). Risk Med.

### Approach B: Re-aim at chatbot and app memory
Same engine, different buyer; end users map onto `group_id` with no schema
change. Rejected for now: it would pivot the entire wedge on one polite friend,
straight into a $24M competitor, and would require rewriting the landing page,
README, roadmap and trial criterion. Effort M. Risk Med-High.

### Approach C: Neutral infrastructure, let first users pick the wedge
Rejected: "memory infrastructure for any agent" is the sentence that gets an
application skimmed and set down. Technically the most rational hedge,
commercially the weakest.

## Recommended Approach

**A**, and the reasoning matters more than the choice.

The write-cost-at-chatbot-volume argument is strong and probably right. But the
evidence under it is one friend showing interest - the exact signal this session
spent an hour discrediting. Pivoting the wedge on it would be contrarianism, not
rigour. Against it sits nine days of deliberate build and daily founder usage,
which is actual behaviour. **Argument loses to evidence, including when the
argument is the adviser's own.**

Approach B is not dead. It is a one-hour test, not a rewrite: talk to the chatbot
builder before submitting.

## Open Questions

1. **The one-line pitch.** Unanswerable until someone else uses it. Do not
   wordsmith it; harvest it.
2. **Which human installs first.** Still a category, not a name. Required before
   this design is actionable.
3. **Does the chatbot builder actually want this?** One conversation settles
   premise 4 either way.
4. **The name.** Amazon owns bare ECHO in India Classes 9 and 42 with a 15-mark
   family. A contested mark during a batch means rebranding mid-programme.

## Success Criteria

Measurable, before submitting:

- **1+ external install**, by a named person, writing to their own graph
- **criterion 6 returns a non-zero reading** - now possible for the first time,
  since PR #22 gave each client its own `agent_id`
- **A 60-second demo video** showing a fact written in one tool surfacing in
  another, labelled with which tool learned it
- **The pitch sentence written in the first user's words**, not the founder's

## Distribution Plan

The deliverable is already published: `pip install echo-mem` (PyPI), with
`echo-memory init-db` making the pip path self-sufficient as of PR #22. npm holds
`echo-mem` as a name reservation only.

What is missing is not packaging but **reach**. Nothing has been posted anywhere.
Candidate channels: Show HN, r/LocalLLaMA and r/ClaudeAI, the MCP server
directories, and X. Note `pip install echo-memory` installs an unrelated product
- that trap belongs in any post.

## Dependencies

- **Waitlist is not wired.** The landing page degrades to a mailto; if a post
  lands, there is no way to capture or follow up. Fix before posting.
- **Site is not deployed.** Domains are owned; nothing is live.
- **No analytics.** No way to tell whether a post produced traffic.

## The Assignment

**Get one named friend to install Echo Memory this week, sit behind them while
they use it, and say nothing.**

Not a demo. Watch them struggle and bite your tongue. Write down the first thing
they do that surprises you, and write down the words they use to describe what it
does.

One action, three blockers:
- converts "interest" into behaviour, which is the demand evidence you lack
- puts a second agent in the graph, which lets criterion 6 return a reading
- gives you the one-line pitch in a user's words instead of your own

## What I noticed about how you think

- You answered **"i don't know"** when asked to commit to a one-line pitch. Most
  founders reach for the most impressive-sounding option. That answer was the
  most useful thing in the session, because it located the real problem: the
  sentence lives inside users you do not have yet.
- You said the differentiator is **"not only shareable but how it reads and
  writes, the enriched context and never forgets but cheap and better context"** -
  four claims, of which one has a number behind it. The instinct to list
  everything true is the opposite of the instinct that wins a YC application.
- When the distribution-versus-positioning-versus-product question was put to
  you, you picked **distribution**, which I had flagged as the most comfortable
  answer. You picked it anyway and it is testable this week, so it costs nothing
  to be wrong.
- You built a validation gate, watched it read 0/3 for eight days, and checked
  the data before accepting what it appeared to say. That instinct - distrusting
  a number that agreed with a pessimistic prior - is rarer than the engineering.
